## 1. HOOK (title cards)
Every day in Lahore, millions of drivers sit trapped at red lights that cannot see them. Fixed timers switch every thirty seconds, blind to the traffic piling up in front of them. What if the light could think? This is Sabz.

## 2. THE BATTLE (battle demo recording)
Same intersection. Same rush hour traffic. Two controllers. On the left, the fixed timer. It changes the light on a schedule, whether the road is empty or completely jammed. Watch the queues grow on the approaches it ignores. On the right, Sabz. A trained neural network watches every approach and gives the green to the traffic that needs it most. Queues stay short. Cars keep moving. The roads are identical. The traffic is identical. The only difference is intelligence. And the winner is clear.

## 3. THE BRAIN (brain panel close-up)
Here is the brain behind it. Cameras count vehicles on every approach. Those counts become eighteen numbers: queue lengths, approaching traffic, waiting times, and signal state. A deep Q network, trained by reinforcement learning on traffic calibrated on real traffic data, scores just two actions. Hold the green, or switch it. Every second, it picks the action with the higher score. And a hard safety rule sits above the AI: inside the seventy metre dilemma zone, the green is never cut while a car is approaching.

## 4. RESULTS (results card)
The results speak for themselves. In benchmarks against standard actuated control, Sabz won two of three traffic scenarios, reaching a best average delay of seven point three seconds. In this live battle, it cut delay by up to forty seven percent. Less waiting means less fuel burned, fewer emissions, and hours given back to the people of this city.

## 5. CLOSE (end card)
We built Sabz for one reason: the people who sit in the traffic should be the people who build the fix. We are Team Sabz, from Lahore Garrison University. Green means go. Now, green means smart.
