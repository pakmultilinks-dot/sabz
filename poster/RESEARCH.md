# Standee design research (October 2026)

Sources reviewed: VistaPrint retractable banner design guide; UI Creative "36 Roll Up
Banner Examples" template gallery (36 real 30x70 in designs, corporate, marketing,
events, healthcare, food, retail); PrintDrill roll-up size and design guide; Gogoprint
roll-up banner guide; Medium "three-zone graphic map" layout article; Behance roll-up
designer guidelines.

## What real standees actually do

1. **Read in 3 seconds from 3 metres.** A standee is a poster, not a brochure. One
   message, one image, one action. If the design still makes sense shrunk to a
   postage stamp, it works from the aisle.
2. **Three zones, top to bottom.** Top third: logo and brand at eye level (seen over
   a crowd). Middle third: the single headline plus the hero image or benefit, this
   is the strongest zone. Bottom third: quiet, call to action, QR, contact strip.
   PrintDrill's ratio: top 15% logo/headline, middle 55% image and benefits,
   bottom 30% CTA and contact.
3. **One huge headline, very few words.** Headline letters at least 8 to 10 cm tall
   (roughly 180-300 pt at full size), 7 to 8 words maximum. Body text never smaller
   than about 2 cm letter height. Two font families at most.
4. **A real photograph does the heavy lifting.** Nearly every template in the
   gallery is built around one large photo (person, product, city, food) covering a
   third to a half of the banner. Flat vector diagrams and icon-card grids read as
   slides or documents, not as standees.
5. **Big whitespace and one accent colour.** Dark banners (black with green accent
   for tech expos) rely on contrast, not density. Elements are few and oversized.
6. **QR code at chest height, about 10 cm wide, with a short label** saying what it
   does. Never bury key info in the bottom 15-20 cm, it sits behind feet and tables.
7. **Standard geometry.** Roll ups cluster around 80-85 x 200 cm; X-banners around
   60 x 160 or 80 x 180 cm. This project prints 24 x 60 in (2 x 5 ft X-standee) at
   150 DPI, 3600 x 9000 px.

## Why v1 was rejected (honest check)

V1 used an invented "S" logo mark, a flat vector intersection diagram instead of a
photo, five bordered info cards, tiny stat grid, and dense small text throughout.
That is dashboard/slide grammar. Real standees use almost none of it.

## Pattern applied to SABZ v2

- Top: the REAL SABZ logo (traffic light mark plus wordmark, from logo.png) large,
  nothing else competing with it.
- Headline "GREEN MEANS SMART." in letters about 11 cm tall, then one supporting
  line. Full stop.
- Hero: a real dusk photograph (cars queued at a red light, from the approved
  cinematic footage) filling the full width for about a third of the height,
  gradient-melted into the ink background.
- SEE / THINK / ADAPT compressed into three quiet one-line rows. No cards, no boxes.
- Three big numbers only (2/3, 7.3s, 47 percent) with tiny captions.
- Bottom strip: Team SABZ, Lahore Garrison University, 4th International AI
  Championship, QR code with a "SEE THE CODE" label.
- Palette stays ink #0B1220 and SABZ green #00E676. No em dashes anywhere.
