# Expo Loop Video Research - findings applied to SABZ_Monitor_Loop v2

Sources: trade-show exhibitor guides (Signal Inc, Exhibitus, Blumars, Tradeshow Guy),
booth-video mistake roundups (MyPromoVideos), digital-signage design rules
(CrownTV, Appspace, Juuno, ListenUp, SVSU accessibility guide).

## Findings

1. **Loop, not linear.** Viewers join at random points, so the video must be a
   seamless loop of self-contained scenes, not a story with a beginning and an
   end. Brand sting opens AND closes the loop so the seam is invisible.
   (Signal Inc: "Loop, not linear"; Blumars: "Make It Loop-Friendly".)
2. **60-90 seconds total.** 30-60s is the classic booth loop; 90s is the stated
   maximum before it bores. One focused message beats a tour of everything:
   a tight loop on one product outperforms a long all-features video.
3. **Design for sound OFF.** Halls are loud; voiceover and music get lost.
   The message must land fully muted: visuals plus text carry everything.
   Music, if present at all, is decoration only. (Exhibitus "Visuals Over
   Voice"; Signal Inc "Silence, please".)
4. **One message per scene, understood in 5 seconds.** Each scene/layout is a
   single idea, comprehended in under 5 seconds; 10-15 words maximum per
   layout. More to say = more scenes, never more words per scene.
   (Juuno "5-second rule"; Appspace sign guidelines.)
5. **Huge type, sized by distance.** Text must be readable from 3+ metres /
   10-15 feet: headlines roughly 90px+ on a 1080p frame, nothing critical
   under ~36-40pt equivalent. Sans-serif only, max two font families,
   high contrast (aim 7:1, dark background, light text).
6. **Answer in the first 7 seconds.** A passerby decides in seconds: brand
   first (logo sting up front), then the problem, then the proof. Repeat the
   logo and core line through the loop so late joiners still get the brand.
7. **Show the product working, not just claims.** Real footage / the real demo
   in action holds attention; results and proof points beat adjectives.
   Animate the proof: numbers that count up are read as live evidence.
8. **Animation is for transitions, not decoration.** Subtle fades and dips
   (~0.5-1s) between scenes; spinning/flying text reads as amateur. Motion
   should support the read, never interrupt it.
9. **End with one clear call to action, and give it time.** A persistent or
   closing CTA ("see the demo here", QR) that stays up long enough to act on;
   QR codes need dwell time to scan.
10. **Native resolution, consistent finish.** Produce at the panel's native
    1920x1080; a single consistent grade across footage and screen recordings
    so cuts do not jar.

## How v2 applies them
74.6s loop; identical animated logo sting at head and tail with black fades at
the seam (1, 2); fully legible muted, low music only (3); every scene is one
sentence or one stat, headlines 110-300px Space Grotesk (4, 5); sting in the
first 5s (6); battle recording zoomed to the panels and stat count-ups as
proof (7); dip/fade transitions only (8); closing CTA scene with the demo QR
held 5s (9); one cinematic grade (contrast/saturation/vignette) on all
footage and the recording (10).
